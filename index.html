  • Joint for joint, no retargeting

    The hand repeats the glove's 13 joint axes: all parallel in CAD, ten exactly coincident, the middle finger's within 4 mm. A glove angle is a hand angle, with nothing solved in between.

  • Force at the fingertip

    Feedback is computed where contact happens and spread over the finger with the Jacobian. Per-joint springs cancel out in a pinch; this does not.

  • Stiff to squeeze, free to twist

    Once two fingers hold an object, their pinch distance is held by the servos' own position loops. Rolling and twisting stay free.

Mechanism

Linkages put each axis where your joint is.

A glove cannot place a motor inside your hand. Closed-chain linkages carry the servos to the back of the fingers and still turn each segment about the anatomical axis. The robot hand keeps the axes and drops the linkages.

Control

One loop, two servo modes.

A PC closes the loop over both buses about 540 times a second. The glove's servos take voltage commands. The hand's servos run their own position loop. Each choice follows from a delay we measured.

Glove

Voltage mode, not current mode

  1. Current mode removes the motor's own damping. The servo's current loop cancels back-EMF. On the M077 that leaves 0.45 mN·m·s/rad.
  2. A late spring is a negative damper. Commands reach the rotor about 7 ms late. A virtual spring of 0.8 N·m/rad through that delay takes out 5.6 mN·m·s/rad. The joint rang at 6.6 Hz and jumped when released.
  3. Voltage mode keeps back-EMF in the circuit, with no delay. It gives 21 mN·m·s/rad on the M288 and 1.3–1.8 on the M077. We cancel it in software while the hand moves freely, so the glove stays light, and let it act once contact is detected.

Damping available against damping a delayed spring removes

mN·m·s/rad, measured on our servos. The dashed line is K·T for K = 0.8 N·m/rad, T = 7 ms.

The M077 joints (PIP, IP) stay below the line even in voltage mode. They get a lower stiffness cap and a 15 Hz torque filter.

Hand

On-motor position loop, not a torque loop on the PC

  1. A torque loop through the bus is too slow. Closed on the PC it sees about 14 ms round trip and self-oscillates above 8 Hz.
  2. The servo's own loop has no bus in it. The XL330 runs current-limited position control internally. The PC only moves the target.
  3. Force is still commanded, through the target. Past contact the servo turns error into current, I = P·e / 256 mA per encoder tick. We add three times the glove's penetration to the target, so a harder squeeze is a harder grip.

Step 1 · detect

Notice contact in tens of milliseconds

Each hand joint has a model of the current it draws in free motion: Coulomb and viscous friction, inertia, gravity. Contact is suspected when the residual passes 25–29 mA, and confirmed when the joint has actually stopped with its target still ahead for 30 ms.

threshold 150 → 25–29 mAlatency in simulation 291 → 24–43 ms

Step 2 · render

Compute the force at the fingertip

In a pinch, six or seven joints are each off by a degree or less. Springs on each joint add up to almost nothing at the pads. We take the hand's fingertip penetration, apply a one-sided spring there, and map it back through the glove's Jacobian.

p = J_hand (q_target − q_hand)
F = −k p
τ = J_gloveᵀ F
k 1000 N/m, up to 8 N per fingerthumb–middle separating force 0.05 → 0.84 N

Step 3 · lock

Hold the pinch on a wall that follows you

A spring sent through a 14 ms loop cannot be made stiff. When thumb and finger both press on an object, their seven glove joints switch to the servos' position loop. Every cycle the wall is reset to the glove's current pose with only the pad distance restored, weighted by each motor's torque constant so the reaction is a pure force along the pinch line.

q_wall ← q − (w∘c) · c·(q − q_wall) / c·(w∘c)
c = ∂d/∂q, the pad distance's gradient · w = 1/K_t
tangential error while locked 0.25° (95th pct)object rotation per pinch, feedback vs none: index 32° vs 22°, middle 45° vs 41°, ring 34° vs 25°

Step 4 · admit

Lock only a real grasp

Three checks came out of replaying 71 recorded locks. Both fingers must press toward each other. There must be something between the hand's fingertips, judged by the signed gap between their CAD hulls. And the lock releases when the object is gone, judged by a signed aperture so that a hard squeeze is never mistaken for it.

zero offsets from self-contact: glove 0.5 mm, hand 1.1 mm RMS

Teleoperation

With feedback, a lighter grip.

A person wears the glove and drives the robot hand. We recorded two tasks, picking up potato chips and picking blueberries into a bowl, each with feedback on and with it off. Line every pinch up at the moment the fingers close, and the difference shows in the motor currents on both sides.

Teleoperated · a person wears the glove

Task 1: potato chips, thumb and ring finger

A chip is light, stiff and brittle, and it lies flat on the box. The hand pinches it between the thumb and the ring finger and carries it to a bowl. 140 demonstrations, 70 with feedback on and 70 with it off, two chips each. While they were recorded, no chip broke with feedback on; five broke with it off.
